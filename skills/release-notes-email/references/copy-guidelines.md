# Copy Guidelines

## Internal team framing (default)

When writing for internal teams at Savoya:
- **TCs (Travel Coordinators)** = referred to as "clients" or "your clients"
- **Viewers** = referred to as "stakeholders" — always list specific types on first mention
- **Stakeholder types**: other TCs, passengers (Pax), security teams, group contacts, coordinators, executive assistants, family members
- **Sharers**: TCs, Company Managers, Team Members

### First mention pattern
Always explicitly list who stakeholders are on the first mention:
> "Your clients can now give real-time trip visibility to the people who need it — other TCs, passengers, security teams, group contacts, and coordinators."

### Role clarity card
When a feature involves different roles, include a "Who can share / Who can view" card:
- Left column: "WHO CAN SHARE" — list sharer roles
- Right column: "WHO CAN VIEW" — list viewer roles
- Use ▶ symbols for list items

### App vs portal distinction
Always clarify availability:
- App-only: "📱 Available exclusively on the Savoya app — not yet on the Client Portal"
- Portal-only: "🖥 Available on the Client Portal — not yet on the app"
- Both: No callout needed

### App update notice
When the release requires an app update:
> "ⓘ The update will be available [date] on the App Store and Google Play. Clients will need to update their app to access these features."

Use green accent for this notice.

### Lead generation framing
When a feature captures stakeholder contact details, add a CTA:
> "💡 Want to convert a viewer lead into a client? Reach out to Product for next steps."

### Notable bug fix callout
When specific client issues are resolved, call them out by name:
> "⭐ Notable: [Client name]'s clients will now be able to [what was fixed] — resolved [root cause]."

## Client-facing framing (future use)

When writing for external clients:
- TCs = "you" or "your team"
- Viewers = "the people you share with" or specific roles
- More focus on value propositions, less on internal mechanics
- No mention of lead generation
- No contributor sections
- More polished, marketing-style copy

## Tone and style

### General rules
- Concise, professional, warm
- Use em dashes (—) for asides
- Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max per description)
- Step descriptions: one paragraph + 3 bullet highlights
- Badge labels: short, uppercase ("NOW AVAILABLE", "ALSO SHIPPING", "IMPROVEMENTS")

### Feature titles
- Action-oriented: "Clients share from any reservation" not "Share Reservation Feature"
- User-centric: describe what people can do, not what the system does

### Subtitles
- One sentence, max 25 words
- Focus on value, not mechanics

### Step descriptions
- First sentence: what happens
- Second sentence: how it works or why it matters
- Then 3 bullet highlights with copper dots

## Savoya brand elements
- Tagline: "Guided by principle. Grateful to serve."
- Always include as footer, italic, muted color
